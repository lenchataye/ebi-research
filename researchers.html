Lencho Taye is an independent ethnobotanical researcher focused on documenting traditional medicinal plant knowledge in the Bale-Shashemene highlands. He leads the formalisation of this research initiative, including institutional liaison, publication strategy, and development of the digital archive platform.

Working alongside the Ethiopian Biodiversity Institute and partner organisations, Lencho coordinates research outputs designed for academic grant applications and international scholarship programmes, while ensuring community knowledge is treated as primary source material.

His work bridges field-collected data with structured scientific documentation — transforming oral tradition into verifiable, citable research records.

Derartu Taye conducts primary field data collection for the Shashemene Flora Archive, working under the formal acceptance of the EBI Shashemene Biodiversity Garden Director. She documents plant specimens, records multilingual nomenclature, and captures traditional use narratives directly from community informants.

Her internship focuses on systematic ethnobotanical survey methodology — photographing specimens, logging collection metadata, and maintaining the field notes that feed directly into the digital plant archive.

Derartu graduates in 2027 and this research programme forms a cornerstone of her professional portfolio for careers in biodiversity conservation and ethnobotanical science.

Philosophy

Research Philosophy

Our approach to ethnobotanical documentation rests on three principles.

I

Community as Primary Source

Knowledge-holders are acknowledged as co-authors of field records. Informed consent precedes every collection event.

II

Scientific Rigor

Three-tier verification — Field Reported, Cross-Referenced, Lab Verified — ensures every record meets archival standards.

III

Trilingual Documentation

Every specimen is recorded in English, Afaan Oromo, and Amharic to preserve linguistic diversity alongside botanical data.
