Origins

Origin Story

The Shashemene Flora Archive began in the field — with formal acceptance from the Ethiopian Biodiversity Institute's Shashemene Biodiversity Garden to document living botanical knowledge.

Field data researcher Derartu Taye received that acceptance to conduct ethnobotanical data collection as part of her internship programme — photographing specimens, recording trilingual names, and preserving traditional-use narratives from community knowledge-holders.

Recognising the broader scientific value of this work, independent researcher Lencho Taye joined the initiative to formalise documentation standards, develop the digital archive, and prepare research outputs for publication and grant applications.

With institutional support from EBI, Madda Walabu University, and technical development support from EAGLOPEN, the initiative evolved into a structured research platform — not an NGO website, not a personal portfolio, but a living academic record of indigenous botanical knowledge.

The Knowledge Gap

Ethiopia is recognised globally as a centre of plant diversity and traditional medicine. Yet vast amounts of local knowledge — particularly from the Bale-Shashemene highlands and Sidama Zone — remain undocumented in accessible, multilingual formats suitable for scientific cross-referencing.

Existing herbarium records often lack the ethnographic context: how plants are named in Afaan Oromo, how they are prepared, who uses them, and under what cultural protocols. Without systematic documentation, this knowledge is vulnerable to loss.

This initiative addresses that gap by pairing rigorous field collection with structured digital archiving, verification workflows, and open research dissemination.

Research Goals

  • Document medicinal and culturally significant plant species from the Bale-Shashemene highlands with trilingual nomenclature (English, Afaan Oromo, Amharic) and scientific identification.
  • Record traditional uses, preparation methods, and collector metadata with a transparent verification status for each entry.
  • Produce peer-reviewed publications and formal research outputs suitable for academic grant applications and institutional collaboration.
  • Acknowledge and protect the intellectual contributions of local knowledge-holders and community informants.
  • Build a reusable digital infrastructure that partner institutions can extend as the research programme grows.

July – August · Two Months

Timeline & Milestones

Two-month internship programme running from July to August — from acceptance and orientation through fieldwork to final reporting.

Early July

Acceptance & Orientation

Formal acceptance from the EBI Shashemene Biodiversity Garden Director, garden orientation, safety briefing, and introduction to collection protocols under Supervisor Jemal.

July — Weeks 1–3

Garden Operations & Nursery Practice

Daily garden work: potting media preparation, transplanting seedlings, watering schedules, nursery management, and field-note discipline.

Late July — Early August

Medicinal & Indigenous Plant Documentation

Systematic documentation of medicinal plants and indigenous sites — trilingual nomenclature, specimen photography, habitat notes, and community knowledge-holder interviews.

Mid-August

Vermicompost, Landscape & Study

Vermicompost production for organic soil enrichment, Melka Oda landscape documentation, and guided study sessions on species identification with field guides and herbarium reference.

Late August

Review, Reporting & Handover

Supervision review, compilation of field notes and photo archive, final internship report, and handover of documented entries to the digital plant archive.
